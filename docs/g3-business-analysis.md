# Andrelook G3 business analysis

Date: 2026-10-11. Scope: acquisition launch planning for the frozen Andrelook storefront. Labels below deliberately separate observed facts from interpretation and experiments.

## Executive decision

Start with one Estonia-wide Meta Lead campaign aimed at men aged 30–49, one ad set, Advantage+ placements, and no more than three owner-supplied creatives. Use a €3.00 daily budget so even a 31-day month remains below the hard €100 ceiling. Keep the campaign paused until the correct Andrelook ad account is accessible, branded-product eligibility evidence and ad identities are verified, owner creatives exist, and the owner gives explicit launch approval.

## Facts

- The launch catalog has 22 published men's products, all priced in EUR and offered as pre-order with an indicated 2–3 week timeframe.
- Public prices range from €69 to €289. The cold-weather launch core is three puffer jackets (€259–€289), three vests (€199–€229), and complementary light jackets, cardigans, hoodies, tops and shorts.
- The storefront supports Estonian, Russian and English, includes size guidance and personal sizing help, and sends a request into an owner-only CRM rather than taking online payment.
- The existing measured lifecycle is Lead → CRM `NEW` → `CONTACTED` → `CONFIRMED` → `AWAITING_PAYMENT` → `PAID` → fulfilment. Paid revenue is recorded only at `PAID` and test orders are excluded from business KPIs.
- Estonia's population on 1 January 2026 was 1,360,745, including 646,759 men. Statistics Estonia's age table records 198,982 men aged 30–49 and 185,270 men aged 25–44. These are population counts, not Meta-reachable-audience estimates. Sources: [Statistics Estonia population release](https://www.stat.ee/en/news/1360745-people-estonias-population-declined-second-year-row) and [RV021 population table](https://andmed.stat.ee/en/stat/rahvastik__rahvastikunaitajad-ja-koosseis__rahvaarv-ja-rahvastiku-koosseis/RV021).
- The available supplier catalog contains RMB quotes, but those values are not verified EUR landed costs. Current data does not establish gross margin or break-even CAC.
- The authenticated Meta browser session exposed only an Andrei Petrov/Ironroute account structure and ad account `1125779726683573`; it did not expose the declared `Andrelook | Store` portfolio, `Andrelook Ads` account, Andrelook Page or Instagram identity. No campaign or asset was created.
- Google prohibits counterfeit goods and may suspend accounts for egregious violations. Current Andrelook Merchant Center eligibility remains blocked pending owner-supplied product eligibility/authenticity evidence. Sources: [Google counterfeit-goods policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176017?hl=en-GB) and [Google Ads policy overview](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6008942?hl=en).

## Inferences

- Men 30–49 are the most defensible starting band: it retains a large national pool while leaning toward buyers more likely to tolerate €199–€289 outerwear prices. This is a targeting rationale, not proof of buying behaviour.
- Estonia nationwide is preferable to Tallinn + Harjumaa at €100/month. One national ad set avoids budget fragmentation, keeps delivery available beyond Tallinn, and still communicates Tallinn service/handover.
- Puffer jackets are the strongest seasonal acquisition entry because autumn/winter is approaching and they contain the highest-value, clearest cold-weather proposition. Lighter categories remain useful for organic discovery and cross-shopping.
- The main conversion objections are likely to be trust in branded goods, inability to try on immediately, pre-order lead time, sizing confidence, and payment/returns clarity. These are reasoned risks; G2 data is not yet sufficient to rank them.
- Personal sizing help, visible price, exact product imagery, explicit pre-order timing, Tallinn presence and an owner response are the strongest existing trust mechanisms.
- The greatest operational risk is low-quality or incomplete requests followed by slow owner response. The owner should treat new leads during active hours as a priority and respond as quickly as practical; this is an operating target, not a contractual SLA.

## Test hypotheses

1. A winter-category Reel will produce more qualified leads per euro than a general brand creative.
2. Exact-product video for Maya or Tyrik will produce fewer but more commercially qualified leads than a collection creative.
3. Minimal-language creative with an Estonian landing page will deliver broadly; a Russian copy variant inside the same ad set may improve response without splitting budget.
4. Men 30–49 Estonia-wide will outperform 25–44 on confirmed and paid customers, even if its click volume is lower.
5. Personal sizing help in copy will reduce sizing-related hesitation and improve Lead → Confirmed conversion.

Each hypothesis must be judged by confirmed leads, paid customers, CAC and ROAS—not likes, reach or CTR alone.

## Buyer profile

### Core buyer

- Gender: men.
- Age test boundary: 30–49.
- Geography: Estonia nationwide, with Tallinn service as a trust/convenience signal.
- Buying power: able and willing to consider €70–€290 apparel; actual willingness is to be validated.
- Intent: branded men's apparel with near-term autumn/winter relevance; likely strongest for outerwear.
- Preferences: visible model identity, clear price, sizing support and a human contact before payment.
- Objections: provenance/eligibility confidence, sizing, pre-order wait, delivery and returns conditions.
- Trust requirements: factual claims, consistent business identity, clear process, reachable owner and no invented urgency.

### Secondary buyer hypothesis

Gift purchasers may exist, but there is no evidence to make women a primary paid audience. Treat gifts as a later creative/seasonal test only after actual order data supports it.

## Funnel and operational constraints

The public funnel is complete: ad → existing category/PDP → product interest → request drawer → success → persisted CRM `NEW` lead → owner follow-up → lifecycle → paid revenue. No new landing-page template is needed. The largest remaining dependencies are external: correct Meta asset access, policy evidence, creatives, verified cost inputs and explicit launch approval.

The €100/month cap makes the usual high-volume Meta learning benchmark unrealistic. Keep one campaign and one ad set, avoid frequent major edits, and evaluate real economics over sufficient time. Do not create synthetic conversions to influence delivery.

## Profitability data gap

For each advertised SKU, the owner must provide a verified EUR landed-cost record comprising supplier payment, FX/transfer fees, inbound shipping, customs/duty and non-recoverable VAT where applicable. Also provide payment fees, packaging, owner-paid outbound delivery, and an approved returns/defect allowance.

Only then calculate:

- contribution margin = retail price − verified landed cost − payment fees − owner-paid fulfilment/packaging − variable returns/defect allowance;
- break-even CAC = contribution margin;
- break-even ROAS = retail price ÷ contribution margin.

No numeric margin, break-even CAC or ROAS is asserted until those inputs are verified.
